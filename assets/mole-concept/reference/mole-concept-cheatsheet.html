Constants and data

memorise
Avogadro constant NA 6.022 × 1023mol−1 A count per mole. Never a mass, never a volume.
Molar volume of a gas at STP 22.4L/mol Gases only, at 273 K and 1 atm. Meaningless for a solid or a liquid.
Atomic masses — the board this unit uses H 1 · C 12 · N 14 · O 16 · Na 23 · S 32 · Cl 35.5 · Ca 40 amu for one atom, g/mol for one mole — same number, different unit.
Molar masses worth knowing cold H₂ 2 · H₂O 18 · CO₂ 44 · NaOH 40 · NaCl 58.5 · N₂ 28 · NH₃ 17 · O₂ 32 · CaCO₃ 100 · H₂SO₄ 98 · Na₂SO₄ 142 All in g/mol. Building these from the board takes 10 seconds; knowing them takes none.

The mole bridge

lesson 0001
Moles from mass n = m ÷ M m in g, M in g/mol, n in mol. g ÷ g/mol cancels to mol.
Mass from moles m = n × M The same arrow reversed; mol × g/mol leaves g.
Molar mass from a measurement M = m ÷ n Answer in g/mol. This is how M is found experimentally.
Particles from moles N = n × NA Answer is a bare count and carries no unit at all.
Moles from particles n = N ÷ NA A division. Multiplying by 6.022 × 10−23 is not the same move.

Empirical formula from % composition

lesson 0002
1 · Percentages become grams assume 100 g Legal because a ratio does not depend on how much was taken.
2 · Grams become moles n = mass ÷ atomic mass Each element by its OWN atomic mass, never by the compound's M.
3 · Divide by the smallest ratio = n ÷ n(smallest) Keep 2 decimals. 2.01 is noise and rounds; 2.50 is not.
4 · Clear the fraction × 2 for .5 · × 3 for .33 or .67 Multiply every number in the ratio, not only the awkward one.

Molecular formula new

lesson 0003
Empirical formula mass E = sum of atomic masses Of ONE empirical unit, in g/mol.
Number of units in a molecule n = M ÷ E g/mol ÷ g/mol, so n is a bare whole number. A fraction means bad data.
Write the answer MF = (EF)n Every subscript × n, hydrogen and oxygen included. n = 1 means MF = EF.

Equation stoichiometry new

lesson 0004
What a coefficient counts particles, so also mol Never grams. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O is 2 : 1 in mol but 4 g : 32 g in mass.
The detour, mass to mass m → n → ratio → n → m Four lines. Grams never talk to grams directly.
The ratio line n(want) = n(given) × c(want) ÷ c(given) Wanted on top so mol cancels. Write it even when it is 1 : 1.
"In excess" the other reactant decides The excess reactant is left over unreacted and never sets the answer.

Multi-step chains new

lesson 0005
Particles to mass N ÷ NA → n → × M Two arrows, two lines. One combined line loses the method mark.
Atoms inside a molecule n(atoms) = n(molecules) × subscript 1 mol of CO₂ holds 2 mol of O atoms and 3 mol of atoms in all.
Gas volume at STP V = n × 22.4 L/mol Only if the question says gas AND says STP.
Ions from a formula unit n(ion) = n × ions per unit NaCl gives 1 mol Na⁺ per mol; Na₂SO₄ gives 2 mol Na⁺ per mol.

Common traps

where the marks go
CH-S02 Mole ratio applied to grams → convert to mol first, always. Coefficients count particles, never grams. The most common error in this unit.
CH-M08 Multiplying by 6.022 × 10−23 to get moles → particles to moles is a DIVISION by 6.022 × 1023 mol−1. The flipped sign gives a number that fits no unit.
CH-M06 22.4 L/mol used for a solid, a liquid, or away from STP → gases only, at 273 K and 1 atm. A remembered number is not a needed number.
CH-S08 Rounding a ratio that ends in .5 → multiply the whole ratio by 2. Rounding is for noise like 2.01, never for an exact half.
CH-M03 amu written where g/mol belongs → same number, different unit. Only g/mol makes n = m ÷ M cancel to mol.
CH-S05 Balancing by changing a subscript → change the coefficient in front. H₂O and H₂O₂ are different substances.
CH-M04 Molecules counted where atoms were asked → multiply by the subscript first. 1 mol of H₂O holds 2 mol of H atoms.
CH-M02 Equal masses assumed to hold equal counts → only when the molar masses are equal. 1 g of H₂ holds 16 times the molecules of 1 g of O₂.
