⚛️ Mole Concept — Cheat Sheet

Definitions

Mole: Amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ particles (atoms, molecules, ions).
Avogadro's number (NA): 6.022 × 10²³ — particles per mole.
Molar mass (M): Mass of 1 mole of a substance (g/mol).
Atomic mass = Molar mass just by adding g/mol as the unit.

Key Formulas

n = m ÷ M

Empirical Formula — 4 Steps

1. Treat % as grams (assume 100g sample)
2. Convert grams → moles (÷ atomic mass)
3. Divide every mole value by the smallest
4. Multiply to get whole numbers (×2 for 0.5, ×3 for 0.33/0.67)
Example: 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O
C: 40÷12=3.33, H: 6.7÷1=6.7, O: 53.3÷16=3.33 → ÷3.33 → CH₂O

Problem-Solving Pattern

  1. Find M (add atomic masses from periodic table)
  2. Plug into n = m ÷ M
  3. If asked for particles: multiply n by NA

Rearrangements

m = n × M   |   M = m ÷ n   |   n = m ÷ M
