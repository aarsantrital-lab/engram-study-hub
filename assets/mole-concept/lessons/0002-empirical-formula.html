Empirical Formula from % Composition

Lesson 0002 — Mole Concept Series

The Big Idea

Percentages tell you relative mass. But chemistry runs on counting atoms, not weighing them. The empirical formula converts % into the simplest whole-number count of each atom.

% → grams → moles → ratio → simplest integers → empirical formula

The 4-Step Method

Step 1 — Treat % as grams. Assume a 100 g sample. 40% C means 40 g of carbon.
Step 2 — Convert grams to moles. Divide each mass by its atomic mass: n = m ÷ M.
Step 3 — Divide by the smallest. Take every mole value and divide by the smallest one. This gives the ratio.
Step 4 — Make whole numbers. If any ratio ends in .5, multiply everything by 2. If .33 or .67, multiply by 3. Round only at the very end (0.98 → 1 is OK; 0.45 → 1 is NOT).

Worked Example

A compound is 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O. Find its empirical formula.

ElementGrams÷ Mass= Moles÷ 3.33= Ratio
C40÷ 123.33÷ 3.331
H6.7÷ 16.7÷ 3.332
O53.3÷ 163.33÷ 3.331

Answer: CH₂O

Quick Reference

  • Assume 100 g — this makes % = grams directly
  • Watch your atomic masses: C=12, H=1, O=16, N=14
  • 0.5 → multiply everything by 2
  • 0.33 or 0.67 → multiply everything by 3
  • Hydrates (like CuSO₄·5H₂O): include water mass, then find ratio separately
⚠️ Common mistakes:
  • Rounding 1.5 to 1 or 2 instead of multiplying by 2
  • Forgetting to include all atoms (count subscripts!)
  • Using wrong atomic mass for elements like Cl (35.5, not 35)
